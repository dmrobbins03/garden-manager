# Lime (variety unknown, researched as Bearss) - Pasadena research note

Researched 2026-11-19. Default variety: Bearss (Persian/Tahiti) lime, *Citrus x latifolia*.

## What the sources agree on
- Nitrogen only in late winter to early summer; none late in the season (UC IPM, UC Master Gardeners San Diego).
- Limes are the least cold-hardy citrus; Bearss is hardier than Mexican lime and grows wherever lemons do (UC Riverside, UCCE Ventura).
- Monthly checks of new flush for Asian citrus psyllid, ant control, and no movement of citrus plant material (UC IPM; CDFA hotline 1-800-491-1899).

## Key numbers
- **Frost threshold 30°F**: Four Winds says protect below 30°F; UC MG Sacramento says limes are damaged at 29°F, cover at 29°F, lights at 32°F; UC MG San Diego says young trees are injured below 29°F.
- **Heat threshold 95°F**: UC MG Fresno says deep water a day before several days of 95 to 100°F, then as often as every 3 to 4 days.
- **Nitrogen**: 0.25 lb actual N in year 2, 0.5 lb in year 3, in 2 or 3 feedings (January/February, May, possibly June) - UC IPM. Dates used: 02-01, 05-01, 06-01.
- **Water**: every 7 to 14 days for established young trees (UC MG San Diego); every 7 to 10 days in midsummer, 4 to 6 inches a month (UC IPM).
- **Soil pH** 6.0 to under 7.0; nutrients lock up above 7.5 (UC MG San Diego). Chelated zinc/iron foliar spray on spring flush (UC IPM).
- **Fruit** 2 to 2.5 inches, turns light yellow at maturity and drops; tree 10 to 15 ft (UC Riverside, Four Winds).

## Disagreements
- **Harvest season**: UC Riverside says October to December (full ripeness at Riverside); UC MG San Diego says August through March. The JSON uses 08-01 to 03-31, with October to December as the peak.
- **Feeding split**: UC IPM gives 2 or 3 feedings ending in June; UC MG San Diego gives four feedings 4 to 6 weeks apart from February, stopping after June where leafminer is a problem.
